\section{Methodology: Event-Time Decision-Policy Ladder}
\label{sec:methodology}

We specify the allocator as a pure function
$\textrm{decide}: \textsc{BlockState} \to \textsc{Action}$ evaluated
on every Ethereum block.  Three nested policy tiers (T1, T2, T3)
share the same interface, each progressively richer in the
microstructure information it consumes.  \Cref{fig:t1t2t3-ladder}
sketches the ladder and the shared per-block panel
$\to$ \textsc{BlockState} $\to$ \textsc{Action} pipeline that is
common to backtest replay and live agent.

\subsection{Per-block state, action, gas cost}
\label{sec:methodology-state}

A \textsc{BlockState} at Ethereum block $b$ carries the per-protocol
lending APRs $r_i^{(b)}$, utilizations $u_i^{(b)}$, total value
locked $\textsc{TVL}_i^{(b)}$, plus position state (current
protocol, position size in USD), gas price $g^{(b)}$ in gwei, and
ETH/USD spot for gas conversion.  An \textsc{Action} is either
$\textsc{hold}$ or $\textsc{switch}(j)$ for some target protocol
$j$.  The switching cost is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cost}
C^{(b)} = \underbrace{G \cdot g^{(b)} \cdot 10^{-9} \cdot
            p_{\text{ETH}}^{(b)}}_{\text{gas}}
        + \underbrace{V \cdot \frac{\delta^{(b)}_{\text{slip}}}{10^{4}}}_{\text{slippage}}
        + \underbrace{V \cdot \frac{\delta^{(b)}_{\text{mev}}}{10^{4}}}_{\text{MEV}},
\end{equation}
where $G \approx 200{,}000$ is the typical rebalance gas, $V$ is the
position size in USD, and $\delta$ denote slippage and MEV-extraction
costs in basis points.  At a calibration point of \$$3{,}500$/ETH and
$25$\,gwei, the gas leg alone is \$$17.5$ per rebalance.  For
retail-sized positions slippage is $\sim 0.01$--$0.1$\,bp and
second-order vs gas; MEV is bounded above by the Flashbots
private-mempool path discussed in \Cref{sec:discussion}.

The expected gain from switching to protocol $j$ over an expected
dwell of $\bar\tau$ blocks at spread $s_{ij}^{(b)} = r_j^{(b)} -
r_i^{(b)}$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gain}
\mathbb{E}[\text{gain}] \approx
  \bar\tau \cdot \frac{s_{ij}^{(b)}}{\text{BLOCKS\_PER\_YEAR}}
  \cdot V .
\end{equation}
The gas-cost crossover inequality $\mathbb{E}[\text{gain}] > C^{(b)}$
is the universal gate every policy below must clear; the three
tiers differ only in how they estimate $\bar\tau$ and how they
condition on auxiliary signals.

\subsection{T1: gas-aware threshold rule}
\label{sec:methodology-t1}

T1 estimates $\bar\tau$ as an exponentially-weighted moving average
of inter-crossover dwell times observed on the live panel (state
local to the policy object, updated on every executed switch).  The
decision rule reduces to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:t1}
\textsc{switch}(j) \iff
  \bar\tau_{\text{ewma}} \cdot s_{ij}^{(b)} > \frac{C^{(b)}}{V} \cdot \text{BLOCKS\_PER\_YEAR},
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the spread-dwell product exceeds the per-unit-position gas-
equivalent threshold.  T1 is $\sim 50$\,lines of Python, has no
trained parameters, and is the most efficient policy on the
test-window matrix of \Cref{sec:empirical} both in net APY and
in gas spent.

\subsection{T2: optimal stopping with switching cost K}
\label{sec:methodology-t2}

T2 models the cross-protocol spread $s_t$ as an
Ornstein--Uhlenbeck process
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ou}
\mathrm{d} s = \kappa(\theta - s)\,\mathrm{d} t + \sigma\,\mathrm{d} W,
\end{equation}
with parameters $(\kappa, \theta, \sigma)$ refitted by MLE every
$N_{\text{recal}} = 5{,}000$ blocks ($\sim 16.7$\,h) on the rolling
$5{,}000$-block window.  The Bellman value function under a
switching cost $K = C^{(b)} / V$ admits a threshold-form solution:
$\textsc{switch} \iff s_t > s^{*}$, where $s^{*}$ is the analytical
boundary derived from the impulse-control HJB in the OU literature
\cite{kissell2014}.  The initial OU prior
$\kappa_0 = 2.1 \times 10^{-5}$ block$^{-1}$ matches the resilience
half-life of the USDC pool sub-kink ($6$--$18$\,h) per
\cite{krause2005}.

\subsection{T3: Cox proportional-hazards regression}
\label{sec:methodology-t3}

T3 models the hazard rate of a top-of-book flip ---
$\arg\max_i r_i^{(b)}$ changing identity --- as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:t3}
\lambda(t \mid x_t) = \lambda_0(t) \cdot \exp(\beta^{\top} x_t),
\end{equation}
with $x_t$ a feature vector drawn from the signal taxonomy of
\Cref{sec:bg-hft}.  Training labels are produced by the
triple-barrier method of \cite[Ch.\,3.6]{lopezdeprado2018}: for each
block, the time until the next top-rank flip, censored at a 7{,}200-
block horizon ($\sim 24$\,h).  The Cox MLE is fitted via
\texttt{lifelines}'s \texttt{CoxPHFitter} on a subsampled training
panel with $L_2$ penalty $\lambda = 10^{-3}$; cross-validation uses
purged k-fold with embargo $0.01 \cdot T \approx 5.4$\,days per
\cite[Ch.\,7]{lopezdeprado2018}.  At inference time the policy
computes
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:t3-decide}
\textsc{switch} \iff
  \int_0^{\infty} \mathbb{E}[s(\tau)]\,
                  (1 - F(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau
  > \frac{C^{(b)}}{V},
\end{equation}
where $F$ is the survival CDF derived from
$(\lambda_0, \beta, x_t)$.

\subsection{Signal taxonomy (F1/F2/F3/F4)}
\label{sec:methodology-signals}

The covariates $x_t$ for T3 are drawn from four signal classes
mapped from MacKenzie's HFT Table 3.2 \cite{mackenzie2021} onto
multi-protocol DeFi:

\textbf{F1 (lead rate)}: Maker DSR rate plus lagged ($300$, $1{,}800$
block) versions and the lead-vs-top-protocol spread.  Source:
Maker subgraph.  Deferred on the present submission's panel
(DSR fetcher schema fix queued for the journal extension).

\textbf{F2 (order-book dynamics)}: mempool transactions targeting
each lending pool, observable before execution.  The direct
analog of Goldman-leaves-its-bid signals from ATD (1995).
Deferred for this submission per the design-spec risk register
(insufficient historic mempool coverage Nov 2024--Apr 2026).

\textbf{F3 (fragmentation)}: cross-protocol spreads themselves
(the decision variable), pairwise dispersion, top-vs-runner-up
gap, argmax-protocol identity.  This is the dominant signal and
the only family fully fitted in T3 on the submission panel.

\textbf{F4 (related instruments)}: ETH/USD (gas regime), USDC and
USDT peg deviations, top-LP wallet activity.  Deferred on this
submission's panel (gas/peg fetchers absent from the 3-way
build).

The shared \texttt{decision/} Python package is consumed unchanged
by both the offline replay engine and the production agent --- the
zero-drift invariant that \Cref{sec:discussion} returns to in the
cross-domain reading of the contribution.
