\section{Empirical Study}
\label{sec:empirical}

We evaluate the event-time, gas-aware allocator of
\Cref{sec:methodology} against six protocol-hold baselines --- the
passive buy-and-hold of each of \textbf{Aave V3, Compound V3, Spark,
Morpho Blue, Euler V2, Fluid Finance} --- and three decision-policy
tiers: T1 gas-aware threshold, T2 optimal stopping on an
Ornstein--Uhlenbeck spread, and T3 Cox proportional-hazards on the
F1$+$F3 signal families.  An earlier hourly MCDM-EMA cliff-edge baseline (which executed only
two rebalances over four months on the same data) is intentionally
excluded from the benchmark set: it is the wrong-resolution failure
mode this paper is explicitly \emph{against}: the honest baselines are the six protocol-holds
themselves, not a straw-man hourly aggregator.

\paragraph{Active universe: all six protocols.}
The allocator switches among \emph{all six} protocols on a single
unified per-block grid: T1/T2/T3 evaluate \texttt{decide()} on every
one of the $\NBlocksFull$ blocks and may hold or switch into any of
Aave V3, Morpho Blue, Euler V2, Compound V3, Spark, or Fluid.  The
engine is genuinely per-block --- it accrues the held protocol's APR
on every block and deducts gas only on executed switches --- and a
block-level integrity audit finds \emph{zero} blocks on which the
position accrues against a missing rate (every held block has a
real, forward-filled APR; \Cref{tab:timeshare}).  Each of the six
also enters the $N\!\times\!M$ paired bootstrap as a passive
buy-and-hold counter-factual, so every cell answers \emph{``does the
active allocator beat passively parking USDC in protocol $j$?''} for
$j$ in all six.

The six protocols refresh their on-chain rates at heterogeneous
\emph{native} cadences --- Aave V3 every $\sim 48$\,s, Euler V2
$\sim 6$\,min, Spark and Compound V3 sub-hourly, Morpho Blue
$\sim 1$\,h, and Fluid $\sim 24$\,h --- and the allocator observes
each at its native cadence (forward-filled between updates), exactly
the information set a live per-block agent has.  We therefore do
\emph{not} claim per-block rate \emph{resolution} for the three
slower venues; we claim a per-block \emph{decision} loop that acts
on the most-recently-observed rate of each, which is the realistic
live-execution envelope.  \Cref{tab:timeshare} reports the
time-share each policy spends in each protocol over the test window.

\paragraph{Data provenance.}
The hold-benchmark set covers $\sim\$36$\,B of \$54\,B addressable
TVL ($\sim 67\%$ of Ethereum-L1 USDC-lending).  Per-protocol
data sources, deliberately heterogeneous to avoid a single-vendor
dependence:
(i) \textbf{Aave V3, Morpho Blue, Euler V2} — per-block panel built
in \Cref{sec:methodology};
(ii) \textbf{Compound V3} — verified RPC fetcher via Comet
view-function calls (\path{compound_v3_usdc.parquet});
(iii) \textbf{Spark / SparkLend} — Sky Messari subgraph
(\path{spark_hourly.parquet}, 6{,}189 hourly snapshots,
Nov 2024 -- Apr 2026);
(iv) \textbf{Fluid Finance} — DeFiLlama Yield Pools daily APY
(\path{fluid_daily.parquet}, 728 daily snapshots).
All six rate streams are forward-filled onto the common per-block
grid at their native cadence (\Cref{tab:timeshare}); the active
T1/T2/T3 allocators switch among all six on that grid, and each
protocol additionally enters the N\,$\times$\,M paired bootstrap as
a passive-hold counter-factual with $N=6$ per-window deltas.  The
policy ladder is run at three tiers; the deployed T3 falls back to
T1 in replay (\Cref{sec:emp-t3neg}), and when wired and tested
out-of-sample it loses to T1 --- a pre-registered negative result
(\Cref{tab:wf-t3-vs-t1}).

\paragraph{Primary inference: walk-forward N\,$\times$\,M paired
bootstrap.}  The binding evidence is a walk-forward bootstrap
across six non-overlapping three-month windows
(2024-11-01 $\to$ 2026-04-30, the full panel span).  For each policy
$p$ and each protocol $i$, we test
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:wf-h1}
H_{1}^{p,i}: ~ \mathbb{E}[\Delta\textsc{APY}(p, \text{hold}_{i})] > 0
\end{equation}
via paired bootstrap on the $N = 6$ per-window deltas
($B = 10{,}000$ resamples, seed $= 42$).  The fund-relevant
binding metric is \emph{net APY} (after gas), not Sharpe: USDC
supply rates have near-zero daily volatility, which inflates passive
Sharpe to the point of paradoxical comparison
(\cite[Ch.~4]{lopezdeprado2018}); the dual-lens treatment appears
in \Cref{tab:wf-nxm}.

\paragraph{Secondary inference: monthly H$_1$ on test window.}
The four-month held-out test slice (\DataStart\ to \DataEnd) is
preserved as a secondary surface for completeness and continuity
with pre-registration.  Reported via \Cref{tab:h1-monthly} but
labelled as expected-low-power on $N=\HOneAuxNMonths$ monthly
observations.  Multiplicity adjustment across the joint set of
walk-forward contrasts uses the Deflated Sharpe Ratio of
\cite[Ch.~14.7.3]{lopezdeprado2018}.

\subsection{Data and splits}
\label{sec:emp-data}

The per-block panel covers 2024-11-01 to 2026-04-30 at the native
Ethereum cadence (12\,s post-PoS), $\NBlocksFull$ blocks across all
\emph{six} protocols (Aave V3, Compound V3, Spark, Morpho Blue,
Euler V2, Fluid), each forward-filled from its native refresh cadence
onto the common grid (\Cref{tab:timeshare}).  Per-protocol block
coverage is $\geq 98.5\%$ with no mid-window gaps; the small
shortfalls for Compound V3 ($98.5\%$) and Spark ($99.6\%$) sit at the
panel's leading edge, before each venue's first observed rate.
Construction of the per-block panel is documented in
\Cref{sec:methodology}; the artifact lives at
\path{data/cached/per_block_panel.parquet}.

The chronological split is locked.  Training: 2024-11-01 to
2025-08-31 (rolling-window calibration only).  Validation:
2025-09-01 to 2025-12-31.  \textbf{Test:} \DataStart\ to \DataEnd,
$\NBlocks$ blocks.  The test window deliberately straddles the
2026-Q1 $\to$ 2026-Q2 regime transition with realised spread
volatility jumping from $0.57$\,pp to $3.6$\,pp --- an adversarial
split that tests transferability under distribution shift in
keeping with the AFML purged-CV philosophy of
\cite{lopezdeprado2018}.

\subsection{T3 ML tier: a pre-registered negative result}
\label{sec:emp-t3neg}

We trained T3's Cox proportional-hazards model with full L\'opez de
Prado AFML rigour (triple-barrier labels, purged 5-fold CV with
embargo $\approx 5.4$ days, sample-uniqueness weighting, L2 ridge
sweep), reaching out-of-fold C-index $0.563$ (F3 only) and $0.582$
(F1$+$F3); the Maker-DSR delta is the only non-F3 covariate with a
positive concordance lift.  Concordance, however, did not translate
into allocation skill.  Two integrity findings, both reproduced from
the code, forced a full re-evaluation:

\textbf{(i) The deployed T3 never executed its model.}  In replay,
\texttt{T3HazardPolicy} reads its F1 features from the live block
state; the panel ships those columns under different names, so the
feature vector is unavailable and the policy \emph{falls back to T1}
on every block (\texttt{decision/t3\_hazard.py}).  Every ``T3'' row
in a backtest was therefore T1.  A previously-reported $+7.03$\,bp
``H1c'' figure came not from the allocator but from an out-of-band
hazard-ranking on a leaky full-panel fit; \textbf{we retract it}.

\textbf{(ii) Wired and tested honestly, T3 loses.}  We (a) merged the
real F1 lead features into the state so the Cox model genuinely
evaluates, and (b) replaced the leaky full-panel fit with an
\emph{expanding-window} walk-forward --- for each window, train
strictly on prior blocks (purged by horizon$+$embargo), then
evaluate out-of-sample.  The honest result (\Cref{tab:wf-t3-vs-t1})
is a \emph{loss}: T3 underperforms T1 by mean
$\Delta\textsc{APY} = \TThreeMeanDeltaBp$\,bp
(95\,\% CI $[\TThreeCILow, \TThreeCIHigh]$,
$\TThreeWins/\TThreeNWindows$ windows).  The model-driven dwell
estimate mis-times switches relative to T1's EWMA dwell.  The
scientific reading is clean: the F3 cross-protocol spread \emph{is}
the decision variable, the gas-aware threshold T1 captures it, and
the hazard layer adds nothing out-of-sample --- a pre-registered
negative result that pre-empts the ``if T3$\equiv$T1, why ML?''
question.

\subsection{Gas-sensitivity and real gas}
\label{sec:emp-gas}

The backtest uses a flat $25$\,gwei placeholder (real gas history was
unavailable at submission time).  We bound the resulting uncertainty
two ways.  First, a \emph{sensitivity sweep} re-runs the policies at
$10$--$200$\,gwei: T1's net APY degrades gracefully from $5.24$\,\%
($134$ rebalances) to $4.17$\,\% ($6$ rebalances) and \emph{still
beats passive Aave ($3.26$\,\%) at $200$\,gwei} --- the gas-aware
gate throttles switching correctly.  T2, by contrast, rebalances
$\sim 159$ times at \emph{every} gas level and collapses to
$0.31$\,\% at $200$\,gwei: only T1 is meaningfully gas-aware.
Second, we fetched \emph{real} historical gas over the panel via
\texttt{eth\_feeHistory} on a free archive RPC (no key): mean
$3.7$\,gwei, and only $\sim 0.3$\,gwei across the Jan--Apr 2026 test
window --- $50$--$100\times$ below the placeholder, reflecting the
post-Dencun collapse in L1 base fees.  Re-running T1 with real
forward-filled gas raises its edge over passive Aave to
$+2.25$\,pp (from $+1.77$\,pp under the placeholder), with $311$
rebalances costing only \$61.  So the placeholder is a
\emph{pessimistic} bound; the honest net-of-gas edge is larger.  In
the current ultra-low-gas regime the gas gate is near-moot and the
binding execution cost at size shifts to slippage/MEV
(\Cref{sec:limitations}); gas-awareness binds in high-gas spikes
(2024-Q4 mean $18.5$\,gwei) where T1's throttling beats T2's naivety.

\subsection{Regime-conditional breakdown}
\label{sec:emp-regime}

journal-extension story.

\subsection{Signal-class contribution}
\label{sec:emp-ablation}

The MacKenzie Table~3.2 signal taxonomy mapping to F1 (lead-rate),
F3 (fragmentation), and F4 (related instruments) is fully implemented
in the \texttt{decision/features/} package and unit-tested against
the research-side builders; F2 (order-book dynamics) is deferred
per design-spec risk R1.  The headline ablation (above) returns
out-of-fold C-index $0.563$ (F3 only), $0.582$ (F1$+$F3), $0.563$
(F3$+$F4), $0.582$ (F1$+$F3$+$F4): F3 dominates as predicted by
the cross-protocol cointegration literature
\cite{gudgeon2020loanable}, F1 DSR delta contributes the only
non-F3 concordance lift ($+1.9$\,pp), and F4 contributes
$\sim 0$\,pp above F3 alone --- the substantive negative finding
for one of the three live MacKenzie signal classes in this
