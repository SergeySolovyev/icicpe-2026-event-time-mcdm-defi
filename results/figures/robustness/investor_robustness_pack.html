The short version

The edge is a reaction to an observable, not a forecast — so there is nothing to overfit.

At every block, hold the highest USDC supply rate net of gas. That rule has essentially no free parameters and predicts nothing. Five independent tests say the same thing:

  • Every window, not one period. 6 non-overlapping quarters → we beat the best venue chosen in hindsight in all 6 (+0.45 to +1.85 pp), and passive Aave by +2.1 to +9.2 pp.
  • The proper overfitting test passes. Combinatorial CSCV — Probability of Backtest Overfitting = 0.000; the in-sample-best strategy is the out-of-sample-best in 100% of 70 splits.
  • We caught our own overfit. Our ML version looked +7.0 bp in-sample and pre-registered as a likely negative — out-of-sample it loses −6.0 bp in 0/5 purged windows. We ship the dumb rule, not the ML one.
  • Selection is the alpha, not churn. Against 5,000 allocators that trade at the same times and pay the same gas but pick venues at random, we are 9σ out.
  • No knife-edge tuning. The single knob is a flat plateau: 5.36–5.37% net APY across 30 settings.
✓
And we show you the two tests that came back ambiguous (a time-shuffle null and a degenerate single-parameter PBO) with an honest explanation of why — §9 and §4. We are not hiding the ugly cells.

Honest footnote — the degenerate cell

Run naively over the router's single smoothing knob (5 dwell values), CSCV returns PBO = 1.0 — which looks catastrophic. It is an artifact: those 5 settings differ by < 0.02 pp (see §5), so "ranking" them is ranking noise, and noise sign mean-reverts across the partition. PBO is meaningful over materially different strategies — not over a flat plateau. We report both numbers rather than quietly drop the one that needs explaining.

⚑
PBO over a flat surface ⇒ 1.0 by construction. PBO over real alternatives ⇒ 0.0. The second is the one that answers your question.

Exhibit 7 · robustness to the one economic knob DEGRADES GRACEFULLY

07The edge isn't an artifact of one assumed gas price

Sweep the transaction cost from 10 to 200 gwei. The router's expected-gain->-gas gate fires less often as gas rises (125 → 6 rebalances) and net APY degrades gently. A naive "always chase the top rate" router pays the same 424 rebalances regardless and collapses through zero. The gate is the mechanism — demonstrated under a swept control variable, not asserted.

✓
Effects that survive and decay monotonically under a swept parameter are structural. The router stays profitable (4.1%) even at a punitive 200 gwei; the ungated version is −7.6%. The cost gate does real economic work.

Exhibit 9 · mechanism DIAGNOSTIC, NOT A NULL

09The time-shuffle that "failed" — and why that's the point

We circularly shuffled each venue's rate series in time (N=300). The intent: destroy any temporal structure and watch the edge die. It didn't die (p≈0.97). Naively that looks like a failed test. It isn't — it's a fingerprint of the mechanism.

Shuffling each series independently preserves every block's cross-sectional spread — and the cross-section is exactly what the router reads. So the edge survives because the router never needed the time order: it reacts to the current observable, not a forecast of the future.

◆
A time-shuffle is the right null for "is this a temporal forecast?" — and the answer is a clean no. There is no time-series model here to overfit. The null that does isolate the skill is the selection null in §3, which the router passes at 9σ.

What we do NOT claim

The honest boundary of the result

  • This is not a forecasting alpha. It is gas-aware capture of a contemporaneous cross-sectional spread. If the six venues' rates converge, the edge shrinks — by construction.
  • Capacity is finite. Edge is measured at $1M. At $10–25M, depth and rate-impact compress it (continuous-supply model: roughly +3.7–3.9 pp retained; the per-rebalance AMM-toll model was wrong and is deprecated).
  • ~18 months of history, one chain. Six venues on Ethereum mainnet. It has not lived through a 2022-style depeg/cascade in this exact venue set; the supply-only design limits but does not erase tail risk.
  • Single-period statistics are thin where we say so. The Sharpe-delta tests over the 4-month test window (H1a/H1b) have CIs that cross zero; we report them as such rather than as wins. We do not headline a "Deflated Sharpe passed" — Sharpe is the wrong binding metric for a near-zero-volatility supply yield (net APY after gas is), so we lean on the block bootstrap in §8, not on Sharpe.
  • T3 (ML) is shelved, not deleted. With more venues / longer history it may eventually add signal; today it does not, and we don't bill for it.
The physicist's hardest attacks

Questions we expect — and the answers

You tested on 18 months. Isn't 6 windows still "one regime"?
The 6 windows span a 10× volatility range and a genuine regime flip (Compound-dominant → first Aave-dominant quarter in 2025 Q4). The edge is positive across all of them because it doesn't depend on the regime — it reads whichever venue currently pays most. A regime change is the router's input, not its failure mode.
PBO = 1.0 over the dwell parameter is alarming. Explain.
It's a degenerate case: the 5 dwell settings differ by <0.02 pp, so CSCV is ranking noise, whose sign anti-correlates across a partition → PBO → 1 mechanically. Over materially different strategies (§4) PBO = 0. We show you both numbers; the flat plateau in §5 is the same fact stated as a strength.
A time-shuffle didn't kill the edge (p=0.97). Doesn't that mean it's noise?
The opposite. Independent shuffles preserve each block's cross-section, which is the router's entire input. Survival proves there's no temporal forecast to overfit. The null that does target the mechanism — randomising the venue choice — kills the edge at 9σ (§3).
Your p<1e-4 resamples 6 autocorrelated windows as if IID. What's your effective N?
Fair, and we measured it (§8). The 6 walk-forward windows are non-overlapping, so the cross-window means are ~independent — effective N at that level is ≈6, and the sign is 6/6. Within the test window, a moving-block bootstrap (5-day blocks) on daily excess returns gives N_eff ≈ 24 vs Aave / 30 vs the best hold, and the CI still excludes zero ([0.39, 0.76] and [0.08, 0.22] bp/day). We're not hiding behind an inflated N.
You have an ML model. Why isn't that the product?
Because we tested it honestly and it loses out-of-sample (−6 bp, 0/5 windows, §2). Selling the +7 bp in-sample number would be the textbook overfitting mistake. The product is the parameter-light reactive rule that survives every test in this pack.
