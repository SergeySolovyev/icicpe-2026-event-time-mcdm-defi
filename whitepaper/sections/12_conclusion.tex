\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection{Summary of Findings}
\label{sec:summary}

We tested whether a domain-aware dual-branch recurrent network
(DA-BiGRU-CNN) calibrated for LOB mid-price prediction
\cite{solovev2026lob} transfers --- without architectural change ---
to DeFi USDC lending-rate forecasting on Aave V3 and Compound V3,
and whether the resulting forecast yields a Sharpe improvement
over a reactive EMA-smoothed MCDM allocator on a binding four-month
held-out test window. Under the same composite loss
(\cref{eq:loss}) and the substitution of the domain-natural
decomposition pair from (price, volume) to
$(\varepsilon = r-f_{\text{kink}}(u),\, u+\text{ctx})$, the network
trains stably on the $13{,}096$-hour Aave panel and attains
validation weighted-Pearson $0.747$ on Aave with directional
accuracy $0.739$ --- a non-trivial in-distribution signal that
confirms the architectural transfer hypothesis of
\cref{sec:disc-arch}. The pre-registered H1 test on the binding
test fold, however, returns
$\Delta\textsc{Sharpe}=\SharpeDelta{}$ with $95\%$ CI
$[\BootstrapCILow{},\,\BootstrapCIHigh{}]$ and
$p=\BootstrapPvalue{}$: \emph{fail to reject $H_0$}. The
hysteresis ablation (\cref{sec:defi-ablation-narrative}) rules out
a mis-tuned threshold as the cause; the documented
2025-Q3-to-Q4 regime inversion of \cref{sec:regime-empirical} is
the proximate explanation.

\subsection{Contribution at Three Layers}
\label{sec:contribution-layers}

We report the negative H1 verdict honestly per the H0-publishable
protocol pre-registered in \cref{sec:contributions}; the
contribution stands at three layers, each of which is independently
defensible. At the \emph{architectural layer}, identical wiring
carries non-trivial signal across two structurally different
financial markets (microsecond-resolution order-book microstructure
and hour-resolution lending-rate dynamics), supporting the
DA-BiGRU-CNN template as portable across the (price, volume) /
(residual, utilization) decomposition pair. The kink-prior
extension of \cref{sec:dual-branch} is, in our reading, the first
architecturally-encoded use of a protocol-published rate model as a
hard prior in a deep DeFi-rate forecaster. At the
\emph{infrastructure layer}, the open-source Compound V3 base-rate
loader and the \texttt{BaseLendingAllocationStrategy} abstraction
upstream-able to \texttt{fractal-defi} \cite{krestenko2026basis}
(documented in \cref{sec:contributions-detail,app:prs}) are, to
our knowledge, the first multi-protocol lending-allocation
primitive in that substrate; both are ready for upstream PR
independent of the H1 outcome. At the \emph{empirical layer}, the
falsified LOB-to-DeFi-lending strategic transfer ---
mirroring the ``negative ensemble'' finding of
\cite{solovev2026lob}~\S5.5 one layer up the stack
(\cref{sec:disc-strat}) --- is itself publishable evidence that
complements the equilibrium impossibility result of
\cite{halpern2024fairrates}: it provides the directly-measured
short-horizon regime-shift instance that the impossibility result
predicts will manifest in the realised rate stream
(\cref{sec:disc-impossibility}).

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Four limitations bound the present claims. (i) The 18-month panel
covers less than two full macro cycles, and the binding test fold
is four months, yielding only four paired monthly observations for
the bootstrap of \cref{sec:defi-h1-narrative}; a longer test fold
would tighten the CI without changing the sign of the contrast.
(ii) The two-protocol scope is deliberate but excludes Morpho,
Spark, and cross-chain venues; cross-protocol structural diversity
beyond Aave/Compound is untested. (iii) The single underlying
(USDC) is intentional but limits external validity to the
stablecoin family; cross-asset rate forecasting (ETH, wBTC, DAI)
is plausibly more or less regime-stable. (iv) The forecaster is
trained once on the pre-inversion window; online recalibration
across regime boundaries (\cref{sec:disc-pred}) is not
implemented here.

\subsection{Future Work}
\label{sec:future}

Four extensions are immediate and each directly addresses one
limitation. First, \emph{regime-conditional retraining}: re-fit the
forecaster on a rolling pre-window at the Q3-to-Q4 boundary and
re-run the H1 test; this is the cleanest empirical falsification
of the regime-attribution argument in \cref{sec:disc-pred}.
Second, \emph{longer test folds}: extending the panel to a
$\sim$36-month window and re-running the four-month rolling H1
test gives a distribution of bootstrap outcomes against which the
present single-window verdict can be properly contextualized.
Third, \emph{$N$-way protocol extension}: Morpho and Spark
integration on the existing \texttt{BaseLendingAllocationStrategy}
abstraction would test whether the predictive edge scales beyond
the Aave--Compound pair. Fourth, \emph{architectural ablation
under regime-aware training}: an attention-backbone variant along
the lines of \cite{wallbridge2020transformerlob} and
\cite{nunes2026lstm} can be tested under the same regime-aware
training schedule, isolating whether the present H1 negative is a
DA-BiGRU-CNN capacity ceiling or a data-regime structural limit. A
Panoptic-style options overlay on the supply position, hedging
tail risk during the high-volatility tertile of
\cref{sec:regime-empirical}, is under active exploration as a
complement to (rather than substitute for) the forecasting layer.
