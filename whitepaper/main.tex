\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We study dynamic capital allocation between Aave V3 and Compound V3
USDC supply markets on Ethereum mainnet and ask whether replacing a
reactive EMA-smoothed rate observation with a 12-hour-ahead learned
forecast yields a significant improvement in risk-adjusted return.
The proposed allocator combines a dual-branch DA-BiGRU-CNN forecaster
--- decomposing each protocol's supply rate into a deterministic kink
component and a learned residual --- with a four-factor TOPSIS-style
multi-criteria decision rule inherited from the author's prior
ERC-4626 yield vault \cite{solovev2026vault}, implemented as a
concrete subclass of a new \texttt{BaseLendingAllocationStrategy}
abstraction contributed to the \texttt{fractal-defi} research
substrate \cite{fractaldefi2026,krestenko2026basis}. We assembled an
18-month hourly panel ($13{,}096$ Aave V3 and $12{,}904$ Compound V3
bars, Nov 2024 -- Apr 2026); on the $12{,}895$-hour overlap we
empirically find a cross-protocol spread with median $-0.232$\,pp,
standard deviation $2.513$\,pp, and an Aave-pays-more share of
$40.7\%$, and we observe a regime shift between 2025~Q3 and 2025~Q4
where this share jumps $39.9\% \to 56.3\%$ --- the first quarter on
the panel where Aave systematically pays more. An initial
sparse-sample analysis ($37\%$ Compound coverage) had suggested a
2025 Q1$\to$Q2 shift; the $98.5\%$ full-coverage panel revealed that
finding to be a cluster-sampling artifact, a methodological lesson we
report explicitly. Five strategies (buy-and-hold, APY-only greedy,
MCDM-EMA, CIR-MCDM, predictive MCDM) will be backtested with a
10/4/4-month split and fifteen ablations. Pre-registered targets are
out-of-sample $R^2 \in [0.15,0.40]$, $55$--$65\%$ directional accuracy,
and Sharpe improvement $0.2$--$0.5$ over EMA with bootstrap $p<0.05$;
the null hypothesis is entertained explicitly and is structurally
falsifiable.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}

Decentralized lending protocols offer USDC supply-side yield that
fluctuates with utilization, market demand, and protocol-specific
governance parameters. Supply rates on Aave V3 and Compound V3 are not
synchronized: they cross repeatedly over time, and
\cite{gudgeon2020defi} establish empirically that Compound borrowing
rates \emph{lead} Aave, with cointegration coefficients implying Aave
adjusts at speed $0.607$ toward the long-run Compound relationship.
A capital provider who can anticipate these crossovers and rebalance
ahead of them captures a yield differential that a reactive observer
misses. On the 18-month Aave V3 / Compound V3 USDC panel assembled for
this work (\cref{sec:data}), cross-protocol APR spreads cross zero in
$40.7\%$ of hours, and a regime shift in 2025~Q4 (Aave-pays-more share
$39.9\% \to 56.3\%$, median spread crossing zero from $-0.218$\,pp to
$+0.183$\,pp) is the first sub-period on the panel where Aave becomes
the persistently-dominant payer --- the kind of conditional structure
that a forecast-driven allocator is designed to exploit. Yet the
production-grade DeFi yield aggregators surveyed in \cref{sec:related}
all act on observed (or EMA-smoothed) rates, and the public research
literature on \texttt{fractal-defi}-based backtesting
\cite{urusov2024clmm,urusov2025dynlp,krestenko2026basis} covers AMM
liquidity provision and basis trading, but not the \emph{lending}
microstructure panel of the same substrate. This paper fills that gap.

The forecasting problem is structurally analogous to high-frequency
mid-price prediction in limit-order books: noisy multivariate time
series, regime-dependent dynamics, a weighted-error metric in which
large moves matter disproportionately, and a discrete decision rule
(rebalance or hold). The methodological transfer from the author's
LOB-prediction work \cite{solovev2026lob} to the present DeFi-rate
setting is the central scientific claim. The transfer is not
mechanical: a hard domain prior (the protocol-known piecewise-linear
``kink'' rate function) is available in DeFi and absent in LOBs, and
the architecture exploits it via residual subtraction.

\subsection{Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Predictive MCDM allocator.} A 12-hour-ahead forecast
  replaces the reactive EMA observation in the four-factor TOPSIS-style
  MCDM rule of \cite{solovev2026vault}, producing the first published
  forecast-driven multi-criteria USDC allocator across Aave V3 and
  Compound V3 on mainnet historicals.
\item \textbf{Dual-branch forecaster with kink subtraction.} A
  DA-BiGRU-CNN architecture predicts each protocol's utilization and
  rate residual separately, then reconstructs the rate via the
  protocol-known kink function. This is the lending-rate analogue of
  the dual-branch LOB predictor of \cite{solovev2026lob}, with the
  kink prior as a genuine architectural contribution beyond either
  prior work.
\item \textbf{Composite forecast loss.} A weighted combination of
  MSE, weighted-Pearson correlation, and a quantile-90 term aligns the
  training objective with allocation-relevant accuracy (large moves and
  the upper tail of the rate distribution).
\item \textbf{Open-source contributions to \texttt{fractal-defi}.}
  Two pull requests of independent utility: a Compound~V3 lending
  history loader with a uniform \texttt{utilization} field exposed on
  both Aave and Compound \texttt{GlobalState} objects (Extra+1), and a
  \texttt{BaseLendingAllocationStrategy} abstraction generalizing the
  multi-protocol allocator pattern (Extra+2).
\item \textbf{Honest negative-result protocol.} The null hypothesis
  (forecast adds no value over EMA on this horizon) is operationalized,
  pre-registered, and entertained as a publishable outcome; the
  fifteen-row ablation matrix is structured to attribute any observed
  effect to forecast, criterion structure, or protocol-pair selection.
\item \textbf{Empirical regime-structure observation on Aave V3 /
  Compound V3 supply rates that itself constitutes a research artifact.}
  The $12{,}895$-hour full-coverage panel (\cref{sec:data}) documents
  a 2025 Q3$\to$Q4 regime shift --- the first quarter in which Aave
  systematically pays more than Compound --- and a $\sim$$10\times$
  intra-panel volatility variation. A subsidiary methodological
  finding (\cref{sec:empirical-spread}): an initial $37\%$-coverage
  analysis suggested a 2025 Q1$\to$Q2 shift that the full $98.5\%$
  panel attributes to cluster sampling, an instructive negative
  result on regime-narrative robustness in sparse DeFi panels.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:background}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{DeFi Lending Mechanics: Aave V3 and Compound V3}
\label{sec:lending-mechanics}

Both Aave V3 and Compound V3 (Comet) implement piecewise-linear
\emph{kink} rate models: borrow APY rises linearly with utilization
$u \in [0,1]$ at slope $s_1$ up to an ``optimal'' utilization $u^*$,
then steeply at slope $s_2$. Supply APY is derived from borrow APY,
utilization, and a protocol-fixed reserve factor. Despite identical
functional form, the two protocols differ in parameter calibration,
governance cadence, and oracle architecture; the result is
short-horizon dispersion in observed USDC supply rates with periodic
crossovers, as analyzed empirically in
\cite{gudgeon2020defi,automatedrisk2025}. The reserve-factor
adjustment on either side is not learned from data: it is published
on-chain. This makes the kink function an ideal hard prior for a
forecast architecture (\cref{sec:methodology}).

\subsection{MCDM in Financial Allocation}
\label{sec:mcdm-bg}

Multi-criteria decision making originated in operations research with
SAW (simple additive weighting), TOPSIS \cite{hwang1981topsis},
AHP \cite{saaty1980ahp}, and PROMETHEE \cite{brans1985promethee}.
Recent applications to cryptocurrency portfolio allocation include
\cite{aljinovic2021crypto} and the asymmetric-PROMETHEE-II hybrid of
\cite{hosseinzadeh2023crypto}. The author's prior vault paper
\cite{solovev2026vault} adopts a four-factor weighted-sum aggregator
(APY, risk, cost, stability) with default weights
$(w_1,\ldots,w_4)=(0.40,0.25,0.20,0.15)$ verified end-to-end on testnet.
The present work inherits this aggregator unchanged and replaces the
APY input alone with a forecast, isolating the contribution of
prediction from criterion design.

\subsection{Deep-Learning Interest-Rate Forecasting}
\label{sec:rate-forecast-bg}

Three recent works directly motivate the deep-learning treatment of
DeFi rates. \cite{agilerate2024} demonstrates 3-hour-resolution
adaptive control of Compound rates, implying that 12-hour-resolution
forecasting has at least as much signal.
\cite{rules2rewards2025} formulates DeFi interest-rate adjustment as
reinforcement learning on a multi-year Aave dataset, including the
March 2023 USDC depeg as a stress regime that we re-use for
out-of-distribution testing (\cref{sec:results}, ablation~14).
\cite{autogov2023} treats governance parameters as the action space.
Classical baselines descend from the Vasicek
\cite{vasicek1977} and CIR \cite{cir1985} short-rate models and the
data-driven partitioning extension of \cite{orlando2020cir}, which we
adapt as Baseline~D.

\subsection{Bridge to Limit-Order-Book Microstructure}
\label{sec:lob-bridge}

The dual-branch architecture of the author's LOB paper
\cite{solovev2026lob} predicts mid-price moves from order-flow and
volume streams via late fusion, in the lineage of
\cite{simonyan2014twostream} and the DeepLOB family
\cite{sirignano2016deeplob,zhang2019deeplob,wallbridge2020transformerlob}.
The structural similarity to DeFi-rate prediction (multivariate noisy
series, heteroscedastic moves, ranking-relevant evaluation) is the
methodological foundation of the present work. The principal
difference is the availability of the deterministic kink function,
which we exploit via residual subtraction
(\cref{sec:dual-branch}).

\subsection{The ``Fair Interest Rates Are Impossible'' Objection}
\label{sec:fair-rates}

\cite{halpern2024fairrates} prove, by reduction of dynamic-repayment
lending to barrier-option pricing, that no fair interest rate exists
in a fully general lending pool with strategic borrowers. We address
this head-on. Our claim is operational, not equilibrium-theoretic: we
forecast on a 12-hour horizon and exploit transient
cross-protocol dispersion, which is orthogonal to the long-run
existence of a single fair rate. \Cref{sec:fair-rates-response}
develops this argument in full.

\section{System Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

\subsection{The \texttt{fractal-defi} Substrate}
\label{sec:fractal}

We build on \texttt{fractal-defi} \cite{fractaldefi2026}, the Python
research library underlying \cite{krestenko2026basis} and the
liquidity-provision papers \cite{urusov2024clmm,urusov2025dynlp}.
The library exposes a typed entity--strategy contract: a
\texttt{BaseLendingEntity} encapsulates per-protocol state
(\texttt{lending\_rate}, \texttt{borrowing\_rate}, balances, gas
context), a \texttt{BaseStrategy} consumes the joint state and emits
typed \texttt{ActionToTake} instructions, and a backtest runner
sequences entities and strategy under a deterministic execution
contract. We pin \texttt{fractal-defi==1.3.2}; the project's
sign-convention lock-in test asserts
$\text{borrowing\_rate}\ge \text{lending\_rate}$ at every timestamp,
guarding against the pre-v1.4.0 sign-flip bug.

\subsection{Entity--Strategy Abstraction}
\label{sec:entities}

Two lending entities are instantiated: the upstream \texttt{AaveEntity}
and a new \texttt{CompoundV3Entity} introduced by this work
(\cref{sec:contributions-detail}). Both expose a uniform
\texttt{utilization} field on their \texttt{GlobalState}, computed
from \texttt{totalLiquidity} and \texttt{totalCurrentVariableDebt}
in the subgraph response. The forecaster is loaded as an ONNX module
inside the strategy and consumes the joint state via a rolling
buffer of 168 hourly observations.

\subsection{\texttt{BaseLendingAllocationStrategy}: the Extra+2 Contribution}
\label{sec:basealloc}

Although \texttt{fractal-defi} ships with four lending entities, no
multi-lending-protocol allocator was provided at the time of this work.
We contribute \texttt{BaseLendingAllocationStrategy}, an abstract
template exposing four overridable hooks --- criterion vector
computation, criterion aggregation, target selection, and the
rebalance gate --- that any future allocator can subclass. The
proposed \texttt{PredictiveMCDMStrategy} is the first concrete
instance; Baselines B, C, and D (\cref{sec:backtest}) are
implemented as further instances, ensuring apples-to-apples
comparison.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Forecast Model: DA-BiGRU-CNN with Kink Subtraction}
\label{sec:dual-branch}

For each protocol $i\in\{\text{Aave},\text{Compound}\}$ and decision
time $t$, we observe the rolling window
$x_i(t)=(r_i(\tau),u_i(\tau),\text{TVL}_i(\tau),\Delta\text{TVL}_i(\tau),
\text{kink}_i)_{\tau=t-167}^{t}$ and predict $r_i(t+\Delta)$ at
$\Delta=12$ hours. The architecture decomposes additively:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Branch A --- rate residual.} Target
  $\varepsilon_i(t)=r_i(t)-f_{\text{kink}_i}(u_i(t))$, where
  $f_{\text{kink}_i}$ is the protocol-known piecewise-linear function;
  predicted by a 2-layer BiGRU with hidden size 64 over a 168-hour
  window of residuals plus the cross-protocol residual spread.
\item \textbf{Branch B --- utilization.} Target $\hat u_i(t+\Delta)$;
  predicted by a 2-layer BiGRU with hidden size 64 followed by a
  multi-scale 1-D CNN with kernels $\{3,5,7\}$, over a context window
  containing $u$, TVL, $\Delta$TVL, gas, and time-of-day features per
  protocol.
\item \textbf{Late fusion.} Reconstruct
  $\hat r_i(t+\Delta)=f_{\text{kink}_i}(\hat u_i(t+\Delta))+
  \hat\varepsilon_i(t+\Delta)$, with a small MLP head correcting
  systematic bias in the additive reconstruction.
\end{itemize}

\Cref{fig:arch} sketches the architecture. The kink function is a
hard, exact, non-learned prior: the deterministic part of the rate is
not estimated from data, freeing model capacity for the genuinely
stochastic residual.

\subsection{MCDM Scoring}
\label{sec:mcdm}

We inherit the four-factor SAW aggregator of \cite{solovev2026vault}.
For protocol $i$ at time $t$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Score}_i(t) \;=\;
w_1\,f_{\mathrm{APY}}\!\bigl(\hat r_i(t+\Delta)\bigr) +
w_2\,f_{\mathrm{Risk}}\!\bigl(u_i(t)\bigr) +
w_3\,f_{\mathrm{Cost}}\!\bigl(g(t)\bigr) +
w_4\,f_{\mathrm{Stab}}\!\bigl(\Delta\mathrm{TVL}_i(t)\bigr),
\label{eq:mcdm}
\end{equation}
with sub-factors
\begin{align*}
f_{\mathrm{APY}}(\hat r) &= \mathrm{clamp}(\hat r/\mathrm{APY}_{\max},0,1),
  & f_{\mathrm{Risk}}(u) &= 1-\mathrm{clamp}(u,0,1),\\
f_{\mathrm{Cost}}(g) &= 1-\mathrm{clamp}(gG/g_{\max},0,1),
  & f_{\mathrm{Stab}}(\Delta\mathrm{TVL}) &=
  1-\mathrm{clamp}(|\Delta\mathrm{TVL}|/0.30,0,1),
\end{align*}
default weights $(w_1,\ldots,w_4)=(0.40,0.25,0.20,0.15)$, gas budget
$G=200{,}000$, $g_{\max}=0.01$~ETH, and APY normalization
$\mathrm{APY}_{\max}=0.20$. The single critical departure from
\cite{solovev2026vault} is the substitution of the forecast
$\hat r_i(t+\Delta)$ for the EMA-smoothed spot $S_i(t)$ in
$f_{\mathrm{APY}}$.

\subsection{Decision Rule: Hysteresis, Cooldown, Gas Gate}
\label{sec:decision}

The strategy executes the three-gate rebalance protocol in
\cref{alg:rebalance}: hysteresis $\theta=0.05$ on the score delta,
cooldown $\tau=1$~h between actions, and a gas-cost gate that
requires the expected uplift over the forecast horizon to exceed the
realized gas spend.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Predictive MCDM rebalance step (per hourly tick).},
  label={alg:rebalance}, captionpos=b]
def step(t):
    s_a = mcdm_score("AAVE",     forecast_a, util_a, gas, dTVL_a)
    s_c = mcdm_score("COMPOUND", forecast_c, util_c, gas, dTVL_c)
    target, s_target = argmax((s_a, "AAVE"), (s_c, "COMPOUND"))
    if (s_target - s_current(t)) <= THETA:           return []
    if (t - last_rebalance_time)   <  TAU_COOLDOWN:  return []
    if expected_uplift * (horizon - t) * NOTIONAL
                                  <  gas_cost(t):    return []
    return [withdraw(current), supply(target)]
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Loss Function: Composite Objective}
\label{sec:loss}

Training minimizes the composite loss
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L = \alpha \cdot \mathrm{MSE}(r,\hat r)
           + \beta  \cdot \bigl(1-\mathrm{WPearson}(r,\hat r)\bigr)
           + \gamma \cdot \mathrm{QuantileLoss}_{0.9}(r,\hat r),
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
with defaults $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=(0.4,0.5,0.1)$ tuned by
validation grid search. The weighted-Pearson term carries over
verbatim from \cite{solovev2026lob}, where it dominates MSE alone on
allocation-relevant accuracy; the quantile term penalizes
underprediction of large positive moves --- exactly the moves the
allocator most needs to anticipate.

\subsection{Forecaster Hyperparameter Grid}
\label{sec:fcst-grid}

A grid search is tracked in MLflow under selection criterion
``validation weighted-Pearson plus directional accuracy $\ge 55\%$''.
The grid varies hidden dimension $\{32,64,96,128\}$, BiGRU layers
$\{1,2\}$, CNN kernels $\{[3,5,7],[3,5],[5]\}$, dropout
$\{0.0,0.1,0.2\}$, learning rate $\{10^{-3},2\!\cdot\!10^{-3},
5\!\cdot\!10^{-3}\}$, batch size $\{16,32,64\}$, sequence length
$\{72,168,336\}$ hours, and a three-point grid over the loss weights
$(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$. Defaults are reported in
\cref{sec:dual-branch} and \cref{sec:loss}; the full grid is logged
in \cref{app:grids}.

\subsection{Strategy Hyperparameter Grid}
\label{sec:strat-grid}

A second grid tunes hysteresis $\theta\in\{0.02,0.05,0.08,0.10\}$,
cooldown $\tau\in\{0.5,1,2,6\}$~h, forecast horizon
$\Delta\in\{6,12,24\}$~h, APY normalization
$\mathrm{APY}_{\max}\in\{0.10,0.15,0.20,0.25\}$, and MCDM weight
vector under twenty Dirichlet samples plus the
$(0.40,0.25,0.20,0.15)$ baseline. Selection criterion: validation
Sharpe under the turnover constraint
$\text{turnover} < 2\times\text{best baseline turnover}$.

\section{Backtesting Protocol}
\label{sec:backtest}

\subsection{Execution Assumptions}
\label{sec:exec}

Decision cadence is hourly; block time 12~s; supply and withdraw
operations are protocol-level and incur no slippage; transaction cost
is $200{,}000$~gas $\times$ historical hourly median
\texttt{gas\_price\_gwei} $\times 10^{-9}\times$ ETH/USD per rebalance.
The withdraw-then-supply atomicity gap of approximately 12~s is
modelled as zero yield on the migrating notional. Yield accrues
continuously per hour at the active protocol's supply rate. The
initial notional is $\$1{,}000{,}000$ USDC, large enough that gas
costs are a meaningful but not dominant component of net return.

\subsection{Regime Split}
\label{sec:regime}

Post-hoc, we partition test hours into volatility tertiles
(low/medium/high) of $\sigma(r)$ on a rolling 24-hour window, and
report all headline metrics per tertile and pooled. Two event-flagged
sub-windows are reported separately: in-window governance events
changing kink parameters (breakpoint reports) and the out-of-window
March 2023 USDC depeg, drawn from the
\cite{rules2rewards2025} dataset and used as the OOD ablation

\section{\texttt{fractal-defi} PR Artifacts}
\label{app:prs}
\label{sec:contributions-detail}

\textbf{Extra+1: Compound V3 Lending Loader (with uniform utilization
field).} A new \texttt{CompoundV3LendingLoader} pulls hourly
\texttt{marketHourlySnapshots} from the Messari subgraph, normalizes
WAD scaling to the same per-second continuous-compound convention as
the upstream Aave V3 loader, and emits a \texttt{LendingHistory}
record. As a uniformity fix, both Aave and Compound \texttt{GlobalState}
objects gain a \texttt{utilization} field computed from
\texttt{totalLiquidity} and \texttt{totalCurrentVariableDebt} in the
existing subgraph response. PR title:
\emph{``Add Compound V3 lending history loader (Messari subgraph) and
uniform utilization field on lending \texttt{GlobalState}''}.

\textbf{Extra+2: \texttt{BaseLendingAllocationStrategy}.} A new
abstract strategy template in \texttt{fractal/strategies/} with four
overridable hooks (\texttt{compute\_criteria\_vector},
\texttt{aggregate\_criteria}, \texttt{select\_target},
\texttt{should\_rebalance}). The proposed
\texttt{PredictiveMCDMStrategy} is the first concrete subclass.
PR title: \emph{``Add \texttt{BaseLendingAllocationStrategy}
for multi-lending-protocol allocators''}.
